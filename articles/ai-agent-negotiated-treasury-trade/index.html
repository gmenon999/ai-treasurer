An AI Agent Just Negotiated a Treasury Trade With Another AI Agent

By Gopakumar Menon, CA, CPA, CTP ·24 November 2026 ·4 min read

At a glance

An AI agent can negotiate for Treasury without being allowed to execute. Keep the two apart, and make every step leave a record an auditor can follow.

The controls in the pilot

  • Instructions set in advance
  • Checks on counterparty, security, size and settlement
  • Price limits approved in advance
  • Terms matched on one central record
  • A person approves before execution
  • Booking in existing systems

What happened

Can an AI agent negotiate a Treasury trade with another AI agent?

Yes. It happened on 17 November 2026, the first of its kind.

Real money. Real trade.

Microsoft’s Treasury agent negotiated a U.S. Treasury bill trade with BNY’s agent. BNY calls it an industry first.

Microsoft’s Treasury team briefed its agent with trading instructions.

The agent reached BNY over a secure gateway.

BNY’s systems checked the counterparty, the security, the size, the settlement terms and the prices available.

Only then did the two agents trade offers and counteroffers, inside limits each firm had approved.

When they agreed, each side sent its version of the terms separately to a central record.

The record confirmed the two versions matched.

Traders at both firms then approved.

The trade was booked through each firm’s own systems.

The agents could not execute anything.

And every step was kept on record: who took part, what was said, which checks ran, and who decided.

The distinction that matters

Negotiating and executing are not the same thing.

Negotiating is where a dealer’s time goes.

It is the back and forth of quotes, messages and counteroffers.

Executing is where the accountability sits.

It moves money, creates an exposure, and ends up in the ledger.

The pilot gave the first to the agents.

It kept the second with people.

I think that design choice is worth more attention than the technology.

Let the agent do the talking. Keep the deciding with a person.

How it maps to the control trail

I read the pilot against the five step trail this site uses. This is my reading, not how BNY describes it.

  • Capture: the instructions and the prices the agent may use are fixed before the agent starts.
  • Match: both sides’ terms are compared independently on one central record.
  • Flag: counterparty, security, size, settlement and price are checked. The agents negotiate only after these pass.
  • Approve: a trader at each firm signs off.
  • Post: the trade is booked in the existing trading systems, not by the agent.

The order differs from the trail: the checks ran before the negotiation, and the match after it. All five controls were still there.

Each step leaves a record an auditor can check: the instructions, the matched terms, the check results, the approval and the booking.

If an auditor can follow that trail, the agent is helping.

If not, the agent is only adding speed, not control.

Five questions before an agent negotiates for your Treasury

  1. Who gave the agent its instructions, and where are they recorded?
  2. Which limits can it not cross: price, size, counterparty, settlement?
  3. How do both sides confirm they agreed the same terms?
  4. Who approves before anything is executed?
  5. Could you rebuild the whole negotiation afterwards, message by message?

If any answer is “we would have to ask the vendor”, the agent is not ready.

Where a corporate Treasury could test the same pattern

Examples only, not a full list

  • Collecting and comparing quotes for a short term deposit
  • Gathering foreign exchange (FX) quotes from panel banks for a routine trade
  • Rolling over a money market placement with an existing bank

In each case the pattern stays the same.

The agent gathers and negotiates.

A person approves.

Your existing system books it.

BNY says it will keep testing, and has named repo (sale and repurchase agreements) and other contract negotiations as possible later uses.

For a Treasury reader, the lesson is already in this first trade: the agents negotiated, and people approved.

When to give the agent more room

Authority should follow evidence, not the other way round.

Start with the agent proposing terms and a person approving every one.

Compare the agent’s terms with what your dealers would have achieved.

Record every exception and why it happened.

Then widen the limits step by step.

Wider limits mean a wider price or size range to negotiate in. Execution stays with a person.

Try this

Take one routine negotiation your team runs by email or chat, such as a deposit placement or an FX quote. Write down the answers to the five questions for it. If you cannot answer question five from your current records, fix that first. Before any agent is involved.
