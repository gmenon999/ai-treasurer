A Bond Trade Can Now Settle While Your Treasury Team Is Asleep

By Gopakumar Menon, CA, CPA, CTP ·26 November 2026 ·4 min read

At a glance

Atomic settlement means the bond and the cash move in one step, on one ledger, or neither moves. That makes settlement faster and simpler. Settlement at any hour adds a new question for Treasury: are the cash, the approval and the record ready at that hour too?

Three concepts to know

  • Tokenised
  • Atomic
  • 24 hour settlement

What happened

Can a bond trade settle while your whole Treasury team is asleep?

Yes. In Hong Kong, it can. Since 25 November 2026.

That day, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority launched a new platform for bonds, the CMU Digital Asset Platform. CMU is Hong Kong’s central settlement system for bonds.

It runs on a shared digital ledger.

It handles the whole life of a bond: issuing it, settling trades, paying coupons and repaying it at maturity.

It can also turn existing bonds into tokens.

Ten banks are the first direct participants, including HSBC, Standard Chartered, Bank of China (Hong Kong) and DBS.

Three concepts are relevant here.

Tokenised

A token is a digital record of who owns what, kept on a shared ledger.

For an existing bond, think of it as the same bond, recorded in a new way.

New bonds can also be issued as tokens from the start.

What changes is how ownership is recorded and moved.

Atomic

A bond trade has two legs.

The bond moves one way. The cash moves the other.

Settlement systems already link the two, so neither moves without the other.

But the bond and the cash usually sit in separate systems.

The link depends on messages between them, and it works only during operating hours.

Atomic settlement puts both on one ledger.

Both move in a single step, or neither moves.

24 hour settlement

The platform settles in several currencies, 24 hours a day, using cash tokens.

A cash token is money in digital form on the same ledger, so the cash leg can move with the bond.

Settlement no longer has to wait for the office to open.

The distinction that matters

Settling faster and being ready to settle are not the same thing.

Atomic settlement makes the trade simpler.

It does not remove the work around it.

If a trade can settle at any hour, three things must also work at that hour.

The cash must be in place.

The approval must be in place.

And the record must match your books by morning.

The system can settle at midnight. Your controls decide whether it should.

What it means for Treasury

  • Liquidity: cash may be needed outside office hours. If your bank settles outside office hours, the cutoffs that once gave the team time to fund an account may no longer apply.
  • Approvals: someone must be able to approve a trade at 2am, within a written limit, or the trade waits until morning by design.
  • Reconciliation: the token record, the bank’s record and your ledger must agree. Three records, one position.
  • Daily cash position: if settlement does not stop, choose a fixed cutoff time for the daily snapshot and keep to it.
  • Access: the direct participants are banks. A corporate Treasury would most likely reach the platform through its bank.

Five questions before your Treasury holds a tokenised bond

  1. Which bank gives you access, and what does it record for you?
  2. What is your daily cutoff for the cash position when settlement does not stop?
  3. Who can approve a trade outside office hours, and up to what limit?
  4. How will the token record be matched to your ledger and bank statement?
  5. If the platform is unavailable, how does the trade settle?

Where it is going

CMU OmniClear, which runs the platform for the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, plans to extend it beyond bonds to funds and other real world assets.

It also plans links to tokenised deposits and regulated stablecoins.

That is a roadmap, not a date.

For most corporate Treasuries, nothing changes this week.

But the question is coming.

Is your Treasury ready for settlement that does not wait for the office?

Try this

Take your last bond or money market trade. Note when it was agreed, when it settled, and which steps waited for an office to open: yours, your bank’s or the market’s.

Then ask question 3: who would have approved that trade at 2am?

P.S. If a trade settled at 2am tonight, who on your team would know by 9am?
