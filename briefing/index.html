From the AI Treasurer's Desk

Agentic treasury stopped being a slide this year and started shipping. Ripple is putting AI agents inside its own corporate treasury to watch cash, risk and forecasts; Goldman Sachs is building agents on Anthropic's Claude for trade accounting and onboarding; Bottomline has embedded an agent directly into cash management. Read the detail and one line repeats across all of them: the agent monitors, flags and recommends, but nothing posts until a named person approves it.

That line is the whole argument of this site. Speed is the easy part now. The harder question for anyone signing off is whether each automated step leaves a trail an auditor can follow, because by November Fedwire, CHIPS and SWIFT all run exclusively on ISO 20022, and the moment AI touches a journal entry or a reconciliation it falls inside ICFR. Faster rails and smarter agents only help if the controls keep up. That is what this briefing tracks each day.

— Gopakumar Menon, CA, CPA

AI & Agentic Automation in Treasury

Ripple embeds AI agents inside its own corporate treasury

Ripple is adding agents to Ripple Treasury that monitor corporate cash, risk and forecasts and recommend actions across liquidity, forecasting and reconciliation — flagging problems rather than acting on them, with execution left to a person.

Reported by CoinDesk and PYMNTS

Goldman Sachs builds trade-accounting agents on Anthropic's Claude

Goldman is developing autonomous agents to handle core trade accounting and client onboarding, framed as digital co-workers that compress the time process-heavy functions take.

Reported by PYMNTS

Bottomline puts an AI agent directly into cash management

Bottomline announced an embedded agent aimed at treasury and cash-management workflows, part of a wider shift to agents that initiate and manage routine treasury activity.

From Bottomline; context via BCG

Cash, Liquidity & Working Capital

Corporates keep building cash buffers

Close to half of organisations had increased their US cash holdings by March 2026, up from a year earlier, as teams hold more cash and manage working capital tightly against a volatile backdrop.

Via Invesco Global Liquidity survey

Faster rails are compressing the float

The shift from cash and cheques to real-time digital payments is shrinking float and shortening the window treasurers have to make funding decisions — raising the premium on accurate, current cash visibility.

Via Associated Bank

Trapped cash tops the stablecoin use-case list

Finance leaders cite cross-border payments and freeing trapped working capital as the leading reasons to look at stablecoins and tokenised deposits.

Via J.P. Morgan Payments

Payments & Transaction Banking

ISO 20022 becomes the only language of large-value payments

With the cross-border migration completed in late 2025, by November 2026 Fedwire, CHIPS and SWIFT all run exclusively on ISO 20022 — and 97% of former MT 103 messages already move as structured pacs.008.

Via RedCompass Labs and the US Faster Payments Council

Banks ship treasury tools at Sibos 2026

Bank of America launched Payments Insights for cross-border flow and working-capital intelligence; HSBC introduced a single dashboard for payments, balances and trade/FX; and BNY opened bank-to-wallet cross-border payouts to other banks.

Reported by Trade Finance Global and FXC Intelligence

Regulation, Controls & the CFO Agenda

If AI touches the numbers, it is now in scope for ICFR

Where AI shapes estimates, journal entries, reconciliations or disclosures, auditors expect it governed like any other system: an AI inventory, validation evidence reconciled to source, change logs and records of human review and override.

Via FinRep and House Blend

No binding AI audit standard yet — but the questions have started

As of mid-2026 there is no binding PCAOB or SEC rule governing AI in audits, though the SEC has begun raising AI questions in comment letters, and broader AI transparency obligations take effect in August 2026.

Via FinRep

Treasury Risk — FX, Rates & Commodities

FX stays treasury's number-one worry

In recent survey work 83% of treasury professionals named FX as their biggest risk, 72% pointed to interest rates and 39% to commodity volatility — a reminder that the hedging toolkit matters as much as the forecast.

Via IOFM

Watch the Q4 dollar-funding squeeze

Commentary flags a potential USD funding squeeze into year-end as coupon flows and dealer balance-sheet constraints cut the capacity to supply dollars in FX swaps — widening cross-currency basis and quietly raising the true cost of synthetically financed carry.

Via FOREX.com

Stablecoins move into the treasury back office

B2B stablecoin payments rose sharply year on year toward an estimated $226B annualised, with the total market around $315B by mid-2026. Leading desks now treat stablecoin balances as a best-execution problem across issuers and chains, not a parking decision.

Via PYMNTS and Stablecoin Insider

Swift's shared ledger goes live for tokenised deposits

Swift's blockchain-based ledger is now live, with a group of banks expected to use it across several major currencies by the end of 2026 to power always-on payments using tokenised deposits.

Via FXC Intelligence

Markets are pricing roughly three and a half further Fed moves by mid-2027, with 10-year Treasury yields seen in a 4.5–4.75% range in Q4 outlooks — front-end US rates remaining the dominant driver for major currency pairs.

About the Editor
