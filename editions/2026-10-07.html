01AI & Agentic Automation in Treasury

Ripple Treasury Expands GSmart: Policy-Governed Agentic AI for Enterprise Orchestration

Ripple expanded GSmart with orchestrated agents for forecasting, liquidity, risk, reconciliation and reporting. Each agent proposes actions tied to policy, waits for approval, and maintains full auditability. Knowledge Studio enables treasury teams to codify organizational controls.

Via Ripple

BCG: AI Agents Move From Insight to Autonomous Action in Treasury

AI adoption in corporate treasury is shifting from data aggregation and forecasting toward autonomous execution. Agentic workflows now route payments based on cost, speed and STP criteria while managing FX exposure and cash pooling, replacing manual bank-portal interactions.

Via Boston Consulting Group

Atlar and Nilus Introduce Production-Grade Treasury Agents

Multiple fintech vendors deployed autonomous agents that handle payments, rebalancing and reconciliation on defined schedules with human governance. Atlar and Nilus released agent fabrics that surface results for review rather than requiring prompts, enabling recurring task automation.

Via Atlar

Via Bank of England

Visa and Lloyds completed a seven-day live pilot settling US$750,000 in under an hour via stablecoins across private and public blockchain. Round-the-clock settlement over weekends demonstrated improved visibility and reduced liquidity lock-up versus traditional banking rails.

Via Visa UK

COSO published capability-first taxonomy for generative AI governance with eight control categories aligned to ICFR and audit requirements. Framework includes risk-assessment templates, testing procedures and metric dashboards addressing hallucination, separation-of-duties and change control.

Via Financial Executives International

AI and ICFR Disclosure: SEC Signals Material GenAI Implementations Must Disclose Control Changes

Virtas Partners analysis of SEC guidance indicates that GenAI-enabled workflows may require ICFR disclosure if they materially affect internal controls. Implementation of AI systems can trigger material-change reporting obligations under securities rules.

Via Virtas Partners

How I think about AI in Treasury

Every automated step should leave a trail an auditor can follow

The test I apply to any AI or automation idea in Treasury: can each stage be explained, evidenced and reversed? If the answer is no at any step, the speed is borrowed against the next audit.

  1. 01CaptureBank and system data arrives from a known, authorised source.The auditor asksWhere did this data come from?
  2. 02MatchRules or models pair items, and the matching logic is recorded.The auditor asksWhy were these two items paired?
  3. 03FlagExceptions are logged and aged, never silently cleared.The auditor asksWhat didn't match, and for how long?
  4. 04ApproveA named person accepts each exception before anything moves.The auditor asksWho accepted it, and when?
  5. 05PostEntries follow IFRS, with the full trail kept for review.The auditor asksDoes the entry trace back to source?

Illustrative framework. It describes a control approach, not a specific system or result.
