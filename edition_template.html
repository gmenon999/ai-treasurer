Start with the decision

Four questions to answer before AI goes into Treasury and Finance

  1. What decision would clearer cash visibility change?

    If seeing a balance or forecast sooner would not change a funding, investment or payment decision, faster reporting adds little.

  2. Are rules enough?

    Much of matching and posting follows fixed rules. Rules cost less to run and are easier to audit. AI earns its place where inputs are messy, such as free-text remittances.

  3. What is the saved time really worth?

    Hours released are capacity. They become cash only when spending actually falls, for example fewer contractor days.

  4. Who keeps the authority?

    A named person approves what an agent proposes, every exception is logged, and the agent can be switched off.

    Questions

    Common questions

    How is agentic AI different from RPA in Treasury?

    RPA (robotic process automation) follows a fixed script: the same clicks and the same rules every time, and it stops when the input changes.

    An AI agent works towards a goal. It reads emails, PDFs and bank files, decides which steps to take and proposes an action, such as pairing a payment to an invoice or flagging an unusual cash forecast. Because it chooses its own steps, it needs controls a script never did: its own identity with limited rights, recorded versions of its rules and model, and a named person who approves what it proposes.

    What is the 5-Step Control Trail?

    It is the test this site applies to any automated step in Treasury and Finance. Capture: data comes from a known source and is complete. Match: items are paired, and the rule and model versions are recorded. Flag: anything that does not match becomes a logged exception that is aged, never silently cleared. Approve: a named person signs off, and the agent that proposes an action never approves it. Post: the approved result is recorded with its evidence.

    Four foundations sit around every system: Govern, Access, Change and Monitor. It is an illustrative framework aligned with the COSO internal control framework, not a certified standard.

    How does the control trail help with Internal Audit?

    Each step answers a question an auditor will ask. Where did this data come from? Why were these two items paired? What did not match, and how long has it been open? Who approved it, and were they independent? Does the record trace back to source?

    When an agent records that evidence as it works, the trail exists before the audit instead of being rebuilt afterwards. It does not replace your auditors' own testing, and nothing on this site is professional advice.

    How is the briefing put together?

    Each morning an automated process searches the day's news on AI, Treasury, payments, markets and regulation, checking specialist Treasury publications first. AI models write a short summary of each story in their own words and link it to the original publisher. A second, independent model checks the opinion lines and market figures before they appear.

      AI Controls

      Every automated step should leave a trail an auditor can follow

      Five steps for every transaction, four foundations around the system. The test for any AI or automation in Treasury and Finance: can each stage be explained, evidenced and reversed? If not, the speed is borrowed against the next audit.

      For every transactionThe control trailCOSO: control activities · information & communication
      1. 01CaptureData arrives from a known, authorised source, with completeness totals checked. Emails and PDFs are screened for hidden instructions before any agent acts on them.The auditor asksWhere did this data come from, and is all of it here?
      2. 02MatchRules or AI models pair items. The checking rules, AI model and AI instruction versions are recorded, and amounts are recalculated independently.The auditor asksWhy were these two items paired, and by which version?
      3. 03FlagLow-confidence results become exceptions. Exceptions are logged and aged, never silently cleared.The auditor asksWhat didn't match, and how long has it been open?
      4. 04ApproveA named person approves, at a level set by risk. The agent that proposes an action can never approve it.The auditor asksWho accepted it, and were they independent?
      5. 05PostThe approved result is recorded in its system of record (the ledger, the forecast or master data), with the sources, AI model version and approver attached. Ledger entries follow IFRS.The auditor asksDoes what was recorded trace back to source?
      Around the systemFour foundationsCOSO: control environment · risk assessment · control activities · monitoring
      • GovernControl environment · risk assessmentA register of every AI use: owner, risk tier, intended use, limits and impact on financial reporting. For any outside AI provider: the contract, how it may use your data, its assurance reports and an exit plan.The auditor asksWhich AI is in use, and who owns it?
      • AccessControl activitiesEach agent has its own identity and only the rights it needs. No shared logins, and bank or vendor master-data changes are verified independently. Personal data, such as payroll and customer records, is kept to the minimum the task needs.The auditor asksWhat can this agent do, and who granted it?
      • ChangeControl activitiesAI instructions (prompts), AI models and settings are change-controlled like code: written by one person, approved by another, tested and logged.The auditor asksWhat changed, and who signed it off?
      • MonitorMonitoring activitiesVolumes, overrides, validation failures, exception ageing and drift are tracked, with a stop switch when limits are breached. When an agent gets something wrong, the error is corrected, the cause is fixed and the incident is recorded.The auditor asksHow would you know if it went wrong?

      Illustrative framework, aligned with COSO’s Internal Control—Integrated Framework and its 2026 guidance on internal control over generative AI. It describes a control approach, not a specific system or result.

      1 · How it works

      Five steps. The agent does the checking, a named person makes the decision.

      A supplier emails: “Please update our bank details.” It is a fake. Each step below shows how it is stopped.

      1. 01CaptureAgentAgent

        Reads the document or request and records where it came from.

        Logs the request, its source and the new details. Changes nothing.

        Auditor sees The request and where it came from.

      2. 02MatchAgentAgent

        Checks it against the records and the rules.

        Compares the new details with those on file and validates the account where the bank offers that.

        Auditor sees The comparison result.

      3. 03FlagAgentAgent

        Raises anything unusual and holds it.

        Marks the request high risk and opens a call-back to the number already on file, never the one in the request.

        Auditor sees The call-back task and the number used.

      4. 04ApprovePersonPerson

        A named person decides. Nothing moves before this.

        One person makes the call-back and records it. A second person approves the change.

        Auditor sees Both records, with names and times.

      5. 05PostAgentPerson

        Records the result and who approved it.

        A person with edit rights applies the change only after both records exist.

        Auditor sees Before and after master data, and a report of payments to changed accounts.

      Source: The AI Treasurer control model.Source: The AI Treasurer, illustrative example.

      How far each function goes on its own

      Each function is counted once, at the most independent level any of its steps reaches. Even then, a person approves the steps that move money, post an entry or change master data.

      • Level 03A person does itThe agent has no role.
      • Level 177The agent drafts. A person approvesAnything that moves money, posts an entry or changes master data.
      • Level 244The agent runs. A person handles the exceptionsMatching, checking and reporting. Never the final money step.

      The rule. Anything that moves money, posts an entry or changes master data stays at Level 1 or below.

      Job description · Cash and liquidity · 6 functions

      Liquidity and Funding Agent

      Managing pooling, liquidity and intercompany funding, and placing the surplus.

      Reports toHead of Treasury (example role)

      AccessReads bank balances, pooling structures and intercompany balances. Drafts instructions and entries; cannot release a payment or post.

      Never does
      • Books or releases a deposit, sweep or netting settlement.
      • Posts an entry without approval.
      • Changes a pooling structure or a counterparty limit.
      Stops and asks a person when
      • A balance falls below its minimum trigger.
      • A placement would breach a counterparty limit.
      • A netting participant has not confirmed its amount.
      What the auditor will check
      • An approval for every placement, sweep and settlement.
      • The counterparty limit check at the time of each deal.
      • Intercompany balances agreed in both entities.

      3 · Go-live checks

      Nine checks: five before an agent touches live work, four once it is running

      They apply to every agent on this page.

      Before go-live · checks 1 to 5

      Put three of the four foundations in place first: Govern, Access and Change. That means a register with an owner for every agent, its own identity with only the rights it needs, and change control over its instructions and models.

      1. 1

        Run the agent in draft-only mode on past cases and compare its drafts with what people decided.

      2. 2

        Treat the text of documents and emails as data, never as instructions. Test with planted instructions.

      3. 3

        Give the agent its own identity, the narrowest access that works, and no sensitive data carried between tasks.

      4. 4

        Name an owner and a person who can switch it off by removing the agent’s access, not by asking it to stop.

      5. 5

        Test the failure case. If the agent is unavailable, work continues by the manual route. If it is unsure, it holds and never releases.

      Once it is running · checks 6 to 9

      Every transaction follows the five steps in How it works. Monitor, the fourth foundation, tracks volumes, overrides, validation failures, exception ageing and drift, with a stop switch when limits are breached.

      1. 6

        Hand over to a person on a blocker, repeated failure or unclear case, and cap the retries.

      2. 7

        Watch accuracy and the override rate. A rate near zero deserves as much scrutiny as a high one. Sample approvals to check reviewers are not just clicking through.

      3. 8

        Treat a new model, prompt or tool version as a change: test it, approve it, record it.

      4. 9

        Keep a decision record: the inputs, the data sources, the model version and who approved.

      Widening an agent’s role

      Earn the next level before you give it. A step moves from Level 1 to Level 2 only after it has run cleanly at Level 1. A person decides, and the reason is written down. Steps that move money, post an entry or change master data stay at Level 1.
