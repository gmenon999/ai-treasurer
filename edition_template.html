      AI Controls

      Every automated step should leave a trail an auditor can follow

      Five steps for every transaction, four foundations around the system. The test for any AI or automation in Treasury and Finance: can each stage be explained, evidenced and reversed? If not, the speed is borrowed against the next audit.

      For every transactionThe control trailCOSO: control activities · information & communication
      1. 01CaptureData arrives from a known, authorised source, with completeness totals checked. Emails and PDFs are screened for hidden instructions before any agent acts on them.The auditor asksWhere did this data come from, and is all of it here?
      2. 02MatchRules or models pair items. The logic, model and prompt version are recorded, and amounts are recalculated independently.The auditor asksWhy were these two items paired, and by which version?
      3. 03FlagLow-confidence results become exceptions. Exceptions are logged and aged, never silently cleared.The auditor asksWhat didn't match, and for how long?
      4. 04ApproveA named person approves, at a level set by risk. The agent that proposes an action can never approve it.The auditor asksWho accepted it, and were they independent?
      5. 05PostEntries follow IFRS, with a record-level trail of the data, model version and approver.The auditor asksDoes the entry trace back to source?
      Around the systemFour foundationsCOSO: control environment · risk assessment · control activities · monitoring
      • GovernControl environment · risk assessmentA register of every AI use: owner, risk tier, intended use, limits and impact on financial reporting.The auditor asksWhich AI is in use, and who owns it?
      • AccessControl activitiesEach agent has its own identity and only the rights it needs. No shared logins, and bank or vendor master-data changes are verified independently.The auditor asksWhat can this agent do, and who granted it?
      • ChangeControl activitiesPrompts, models and settings are change-controlled like code: written by one person, approved by another, tested and logged.The auditor asksWhat changed, and who signed it off?
      • MonitorMonitoring activitiesVolumes, overrides, validation failures, exception ageing and drift are tracked, with a stop switch when limits are breached.The auditor asksHow would you know if it went wrong?

      Illustrative framework, aligned with COSO’s Internal Control—Integrated Framework and its 2026 guidance on internal control over generative AI. It describes a control approach, not a specific system or result.
